# Governance

Access To Experts (A2X) operates under a multi-stakeholder governance model designed to balance founder leadership, expert domain authority, institutional partnerships, and community trust.

## Governance Principles

1. **Expertise first.** Domain decisions are made by those with relevant credentials and practice experience.
2. **Transparency.** Governance processes, decisions, and rationales are documented publicly where possible.
3. **Child-centered and trauma-informed.** Where platform decisions affect vulnerable populations, those impacts are central to evaluation.
4. **Jurisdiction awareness.** Global platform, local rules. Regional stakeholders have voice in regional decisions.
5. **Separation of interests.** Commercial incentives are balanced by independent oversight.

## Governance Bodies

### 1. Founding Team

**Role:** Day-to-day execution, product direction, commercial strategy.

**Current composition:** Doug Devitre (Founder, CEO, Head of Engineering)

**Authority:** Product roadmap, hiring, capital allocation, partnerships, operational decisions.

**Accountability:** Advisory Board and investors.

### 2. Advisory Board

**Role:** Strategic guidance across verticals, institutional partnerships, and global expansion.

**Composition:** 5–9 members, at least one heavyweight per flagship vertical. Target roster includes:

- Retired federal judge or former state Supreme Court justice (legal vertical)
- Former Surgeon General or major health system CMO (clinical vertical)
- Former Fortune 500 CHRO or strategy firm senior partner (business vertical)
- International expert (non-US jurisdiction, priority: UK, EU, Canada)
- Legal tech or vertical SaaS operator with successful exit
- Civic tech leader with government procurement experience

**Authority:** Non-binding guidance, introductions, credibility.

**Term:** 2 years, renewable.

**Compensation:** Equity (advisor shares) and modest cash stipend.

### 3. Ethics Board

**Role:** Independent review of policies and cases with significant ethical, legal, or safety implications.

**Composition:** 3–5 members, including:

- A legal ethics scholar or bar counsel
- A clinical ethicist (APA, bioethics background)
- A children's rights or trauma-informed practice expert
- A privacy and data protection expert
- A public interest technology representative

**Authority:** Binding review on matters including:

- Expert deplatforming decisions
- Playbook takedowns for safety or ethical reasons
- Content policy changes affecting vulnerable populations
- Research data use approvals
- Major incidents involving user harm

**Term:** 3 years, staggered, renewable once.

**Compensation:** Cash stipend; no equity (to preserve independence).

**Meetings:** Quarterly, plus ad-hoc for urgent matters. Decisions documented publicly (redacted where necessary).

### 4. Expert Council

**Role:** Represents the practitioner community. Input on platform changes affecting experts.

**Composition:** 9–15 elected representatives from the Certified Practitioner tier and above, with vertical balance:

- 3 legal (with jurisdictional diversity)
- 2 clinical/mental health
- 2 business/strategy
- 1 civic/government
- 1 education
- 1 international
- Rotating vertical seats as new verticals launch

**Authority:** Consultation on:

- Expert fee structures and revenue share changes
- Content review policies
- QA and peer review processes
- Platform features affecting authoring and monetization
- Dispute resolution procedures

**Term:** 2 years, staggered elections.

**Elected by:** Active experts in good standing.

### 5. Research Board

**Role:** Oversight of data products, research partnerships, and publication of platform data.

**Composition:** 3–5 members from academic institutions, think tanks, and research ethics backgrounds.

**Authority:** Approval required for:

- External research partnerships using platform data
- Publication of platform data products
- AI training data licensing to third parties
- Access to aggregated data by third-party researchers

**Term:** 3 years, renewable once.

## Decision-Making Framework

### Product & Engineering Decisions

- **Small changes:** Founder/maintainer discretion
- **Significant changes:** Public RFC, community comment period, maintainer decision
- **Breaking changes:** Require RFC + Advisory Board consultation

### Content Policy Decisions

- **Minor clarifications:** Maintainer discretion
- **Policy changes:** Expert Council consultation + Ethics Board review for sensitive topics
- **Emergency takedowns:** Maintainer authority; post-hoc Ethics Board review

### Expert Tier & Credentialing

- **Tier definitions:** Advisory Board + Expert Council
- **Individual expert status changes:** Content QA team; appeals to Ethics Board

### Financial & Commercial

- **Pricing changes:** Founding Team; major changes (>20%) reviewed by Advisory Board
- **Revenue share changes:** Founding Team + Expert Council consultation
- **Capital decisions:** Founding Team + Board of Directors (post-funding)

## Transparency

- **Public:** Governance structure, board composition, major policy decisions, aggregate metrics
- **Redacted public:** Ethics Board decision summaries
- **Internal:** Individual expert disputes, user complaints, security incidents

## Evolution of Governance

This governance model is expected to evolve as A2X grows:

- **Phase 0–1 (founder-led):** Current model, Advisory Board
- **Phase 2 (first enterprise contracts):** Ethics Board formalized, Expert Council elections begin
- **Phase 3 (international expansion):** Regional advisory councils added
- **Phase 4 (scale):** Consider foundation or stewardship structure for platform governance separate from commercial entity

Changes to this governance document require:

1. Public RFC with 30-day comment period
2. Advisory Board majority approval
3. Ethics Board review if changes affect oversight mechanisms

## Conflicts of Interest

All governance body members must:

- Disclose material financial interests in the platform, experts, or competitors
- Recuse from decisions where they have a direct conflict
- Update disclosures annually

COI disclosures are maintained internally and reviewed by the Ethics Board.
