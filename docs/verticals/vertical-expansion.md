## Vertical Selection Criteria

A new vertical is considered when it scores high on these dimensions:

1. **Available anchor** — is there a credible institutional or individual anchor we can sign?
2. **Documented methodology** — does a canonical, teachable body of practice exist?
3. **Regulatory clarity** — can we operate defensibly? (UPL, HIPAA, FERPA, etc.)
4. **Buyer willingness to pay** — B2B, B2C, or B2G demand with validated budgets?
5. **Cross-vertical synergy** — does it compound existing user base?
6. **Data moat potential** — will session data produce a defensible analytics asset?
7. **Content velocity** — can we launch with 20+ playbooks?

## Vertical Launch Playbook

Each new vertical follows the same launch playbook:

1. **Anchor lock-in** — signed agreement with anchor institution or expert (3 months)
2. **Vertical council** — 5–10 practitioners, advisory role, shapes first content set
3. **Taxonomy build** — practice areas, jurisdictions, roles specific to the vertical
4. **Flagship playbook set** — 10–20 playbooks authored and peer-reviewed
5. **Regulatory review** — vertical-specific legal and compliance assessment
6. **Beta cohort** — 50–100 users test for 60 days
7. **Public launch** — with press, co-marketing from anchor, webinar series
8. **Scale** — expand to 100+ playbooks, recruit tier-2 and tier-3 experts

## De-Risking New Verticals

- Never launch a vertical without an anchor
- Never launch a vertical without regulatory review
- Never let a new vertical cannibalize flagship resources — dedicated pod structure
- Measure new-vertical economics against flagship; kill verticals that underperform at month 18

## Avoiding Vertical Sprawl

Temptation to chase every vertical must be resisted. The platform's value compounds when verticals share infrastructure and users share context. Verticals that don't benefit from shared infrastructure (e.g., agricultural commodity trading) should be declined or spun out.
