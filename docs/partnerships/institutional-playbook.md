# Institutional Partnership Playbook

How to identify, approach, structure, and close institutional partnerships. Institutional partners are the single highest-leverage supply and distribution source for A2X.

---

## Why Institutions

- **Content** — bar associations, medical societies, and professional bodies have rich CLE/CE libraries that can be converted to playbooks
- **Credibility** — partnership with AFCC or ABA is worth more than 100 unknown experts
- **Distribution** — institutional channels reach members directly with low CAC
- **Regulation** — partnership with licensing bodies provides defensible posture on UPL, continuing education, and professional standards
- **Network effects** — institutions refer peers; partnerships compound

---

## Partner Selection Criteria

Every potential partner is evaluated on:

1. **Credibility** — is partnership with them a trust signal to our target audience?
2. **Reach** — how many members or constituents do they touch?
3. **Content** — do they have existing content or expert bench to contribute?
4. **Mission fit** — does A2X align with their stated mission?
5. **Decision-making** — can we identify a champion with authority?
6. **Timing** — are they in a strategic moment (new director, strategic refresh, technology evaluation)?
7. **Complexity** — is their legal/procurement process manageable for our stage?

**Target scoring:** partners scoring ≥ 4 of 7 high are worth pursuing. Below that, deprioritize.

## Pitch Framework

### Opening (who we are)

> Access To Experts is a platform where credentialed experts package their methodology as guided, executable playbooks. We're built for institutions like yours — where your members have deep expertise that could reach tens of thousands of practitioners at scale.

### Problem (why now)

> Your members are being displaced by generic AI. Your CLE/CE content is static. Your authority is unmonetized outside direct delivery. You need a platform that amplifies your institution's expertise with built-in trust, jurisdiction awareness, and revenue potential.

### Solution (what we offer)

> We provide the platform. You bring the credibility, content, and membership. Together we co-brand a playbook library that:
> - Your members earn CE credit for completing
> - Your institution earns revenue share on
> - Your members can author and monetize their methodology
> - External professionals discover and learn from your institution's authority

### Proof (why you can trust us)

> - Founded by [Doug Devitre's bio positioning]
> - Backed by [advisors as they come on]
> - Built with SOC 2 posture from day one
> - Child-centered, trauma-informed approach
> - Partnership pilots with [other institution names] already in motion

### Ask (what we want)

> We're offering a founding-partner agreement: co-branded library, favorable revenue split, editorial authority on content in your domain, seat on advisory council. In exchange, we ask for [specific content commitment, expert nominations, member distribution].

### Close (next step)

> Can we schedule a 30-minute working session with your content team to scope a pilot?

## Meeting Process

### Initial meeting (30 min)

- Discovery: their mission, priorities, challenges
- Pitch: compressed version
- Mutual fit check: is this worth both parties' time?
- Next step: working session or decline

### Working session (60–90 min)

- Specific pilot scope discussion
- Content available from their side
- Experts they can introduce
- Distribution channel they can activate
- Expected outcomes for them
- Expected outcomes for us
- Commercial terms directionally

### Proposal (1–2 weeks after working session)

- Written pilot scope
- Content deliverables both sides
- Revenue sharing terms
- Timeline
- Success criteria
- Exclusivity or non-exclusivity

### Pilot agreement (2–4 weeks)

- Negotiated agreement (their legal review)
- Typically 6-month pilot term
- Non-binding renewal conversation at 90 days in
- Metrics captured throughout

### Renewal or expand (month 6+)

- Formal review of pilot metrics
- Decision: renew, expand, or conclude
- If renew: multi-year with deeper commercial terms
- If expand: additional verticals or deeper integration
- If conclude: clean exit, preserve goodwill

## Deal Structure Templates

### Pilot Agreement (default starting point)

- Term: 6 months
- Exclusivity: none
- Content: both sides contribute as agreed
- Revenue share: simplified (e.g., 20% to institution on playbooks using their experts or content)
- Co-branding: mutual, with style guide alignment
- Termination: either party, 30-day notice
- IP: each party retains own; license for platform purposes

### Multi-year Institutional Partnership

- Term: 2–3 years with renewal
- Content commitment: both sides, specific counts
- Revenue share: tiered by volume
- Exclusivity: limited (e.g., "exclusive platform partner for family law CLE in Missouri")
- Advisory role: institution has seat or observer on A2X Advisory Board
- Governance: joint editorial oversight

### White-Label Deal

- Term: 3+ years
- Institution deploys A2X under own brand
- Revenue share: favorable to institution (60–70%)
- Setup fee: $25–100K
- Integration: SSO with their member system
- Dedicated tenant (or logical isolation)

### Government Contract

- Per procurement process
- Multi-year with option years
- Fixed-fee or per-seat or usage
- Data residency, clearance requirements
- Specific SLAs

---

## Negotiation Principles

1. **Never lead with price** — lead with mission and mutual value
2. **Offer founding-partner terms in Phase 1** — favorable economics in exchange for early commitment
3. **Protect editorial independence** — institutions should not have veto over non-institution content in other verticals
4. **Protect platform integrity** — no exclusivity that prevents A2X from serving its mission
5. **Structure for compounding** — partnerships that generate referrals are worth more than large one-off deals
6. **Document the "why"** — every negotiated term should have a principled rationale

---

## Relationship Management

After signing:

- **Monthly check-in** with primary contact
- **Quarterly business review** with senior stakeholders
- **Annual partnership summit** (once several partnerships exist)
- **Proactive communication** on product changes, incidents, wins
- **Joint marketing moments** — launches, milestones, case studies
- **Feedback loop** — their members' feedback → product roadmap

---

## Red Flags

- Partner insists on exclusivity beyond what's fair
- Partner wants to own A2X's platform IP
- Partner cannot identify a clear champion or decision-maker
- Legal review is circular or opaque
- Partner's mission drift or scandal risk
- Partner treats A2X as a vendor rather than partner

---

## Graceful Exits

Not every partnership will work. Exit principles:

- Respect contractual terms
- Preserve both sides' reputation
- Transfer or wind down content thoughtfully
- Honor commitments to users who accessed via partnership
- Document learnings

---

## Tracking

All partnership opportunities tracked in a dedicated pipeline:

| Stage | Definition |
|---|---|
| Identified | On target list, not yet contacted |
| Contacted | Outreach sent; awaiting response |
| In Discussion | Active conversation |
| Working Session | Detailed scope discussion scheduled or held |
| Proposal Sent | Formal proposal delivered |
| In Negotiation | Terms being discussed |
| Pilot Agreement | Signed pilot |
| Active Partnership | Multi-year or mature partnership |
| Paused / Cooled | Inactive; revisit later |
| Closed Lost | Not proceeding; documented reason |

Metrics:
- Pipeline value
- Velocity through stages
- Conversion rates
- Time to close
- Active partnerships count
