# Enterprise Strategy

## Thesis

Access To Experts is not a consumer marketplace that grows into enterprise later. It is an enterprise-grade expertise platform from day one that also serves consumers. This posture is what separates A2X from Upwork-style marketplaces, MasterClass-style content libraries, and TurboTax-style consumer workflow tools.

The enterprise strategy rests on three pillars:

1. **Institutional supply** — anchor with professional bodies, not individuals
2. **Enterprise demand** — sell to corporations, governments, and professional bodies in parallel with consumer growth
3. **Trust infrastructure** — build enterprise-grade compliance, verification, and governance from day one

---

## Pillar 1: Institutional Supply

Individual expert marketplaces cap out because supply is fragmented and quality is inconsistent. Enterprise-grade platforms anchor with **institutions** that bring credibility, content, and distribution in a single package. This is how Coursera scaled (Stanford, Yale, Google), how MasterClass scaled (celebrity-brand partnerships), and how Bloomberg Terminal scaled (institutional data providers).

### Deal Structure Patterns

| Model | Description | Use When |
|---|---|---|
| **Content Licensing** | Institution licenses its content to A2X for playbook conversion | Institution has rich existing content (CLE libraries, case studies) |
| **Co-Branded Authorship** | Platform + institution co-brand playbooks authored by institution's experts | Institution has expert bench but limited content |
| **Editorial Partnership** | Institution provides peer review authority over a vertical | Institution has credibility but no direct authoring capacity |
| **Member Benefit** | A2X access bundled as member benefit (discount or included) | Institution wants to add value for dues-paying members |
| **Certification Partnership** | Institution issues CLE/CE/CME credit for completed playbooks | Professional body with continuing education authority |
| **White-Label** | Institution deploys A2X under its brand | Institution wants platform without A2X brand visibility |
| **Joint Venture** | Shared entity for specific vertical or region | Large strategic opportunity (e.g., state bar partnership) |

### Institutional Partnership Playbook

Target sequence for first institutional deal:

1. **Identify champion** — individual at institution with authority and interest
2. **Co-design pilot** — 5–10 playbooks in institution's priority area
3. **MOU or pilot agreement** — non-exclusive, 6-month term, clear success criteria
4. **Pilot execution** — joint authoring, QA, launch to institution's members
5. **Success review** — usage, satisfaction, member feedback
6. **Renewal / expansion** — convert pilot to multi-year contract

**Target first institutional pilot:** Missouri Bar Association family law section, or AFCC, or a regional EAP provider. Revenue goal: symbolic first year ($10–25K), credibility and case study primary.

## Pillar 2: Enterprise Demand

Consumer credits are a B2C play with thin margins. World-class platforms build a parallel B2B surface that funds the consumer side and drives LTV/CAC economics that consumer-only platforms cannot match.

### Enterprise SKUs

#### Corporate License

- **Buyer:** Fortune 500 legal, HR, compliance, and risk teams
- **Pricing:** $10K–$500K/year, seat-based
- **Features:** Curated library access, SSO, usage analytics, dedicated success manager, SLA
- **GTM:** Direct sales, industry conferences, channel partnerships
- **Analogy:** Westlaw, Lexis+, CCH for compliance

#### Government Contract

- **Buyer:** State AG offices, court systems, workforce agencies, human services
- **Pricing:** $50K–$5M multi-year, procurement-negotiated
- **Features:** FedRAMP or StateRAMP compliance, state data residency, custom playbook development
- **GTM:** GSA schedule, state procurement, cooperative purchasing contracts
- **Analogy:** NIC Inc., Tyler Technologies for government software

#### White-Label / OEM

- **Buyer:** Bar associations, professional associations, EAP providers, membership organizations
- **Pricing:** Revenue share (typically 30/70 or 40/60) + setup fees ($25K–$100K)
- **Features:** Custom branding, custom domain, institutional admin, member directory integration
- **GTM:** Association conferences, executive relationships
- **Analogy:** Higher Logic for associations, Memberclicks for AMS

#### API Access

- **Buyer:** Law firms, consultancies, HR platforms, case management vendors
- **Pricing:** Usage-based ($0.10–$1.00 per API call depending on tier), plus monthly minimums
- **Features:** Playbook execution API, session API, artifact generation API, webhooks
- **GTM:** Developer marketing, Postman collections, SDK distribution
- **Analogy:** Twilio, Stripe, Plaid API models

#### Credentialing Partnership

- **Buyer:** Regulatory bodies (state bars, medical boards) and individual practitioners
- **Pricing:** Platform takes 20–40% of CLE/CE fees; practitioners pay per-credit completion
- **Features:** CLE/CE/CME approved playbooks, automatic credit reporting to licensing bodies, transcript management
- **GTM:** Partnership with continuing education bodies
- **Analogy:** Lawline, ProCE models

## Pillar 3: Trust Infrastructure

Enterprise buyers do not care about marketplace claims. They care about verifiable trust signals.

### Compliance & Certification Stack

| Certification | Priority | Timeline | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| SOC 2 Type I | Critical | Phase 1 end | Enterprise sales table stakes |
| SOC 2 Type II | Critical | Phase 2 end | Mature enterprise sales |
| ISO 27001 | High | Phase 3 | International enterprise |
| GDPR compliance | Critical | Phase 1 | EU market + best practice |
| CCPA + state privacy | Critical | Phase 1 | US consumer + enterprise |
| HIPAA | High | Phase 3 | Unlocks clinical vertical |
| WCAG 2.2 AA | Critical | Phase 1 | Government + best practice |
| FedRAMP Moderate | Medium | Phase 4 | Federal government contracts |
| StateRAMP | Medium | Phase 3 | State government contracts |
| ABA Model Rule alignment | Critical | Phase 1 | Legal vertical defensibility |

### Expert Verification at Scale

Multi-layer verification:

- **Identity** — Government ID + live video verification (Persona or Jumio)
- **Credential** — Automated API lookups for bar licenses (all 50 US states + international), medical licenses (FSMB), professional body memberships
- **Background** — Continuous monitoring for disciplinary actions (via public databases)
- **Content** — Independent peer review by vertical-specific review boards
- **Insurance** — Proof of malpractice/E&O insurance required for legal and clinical verticals

### Liability & Insurance Framework

- **Platform E&O** — A2X maintains errors and omissions insurance
- **Expert requirements** — Malpractice insurance mandatory for legal and clinical verticals
- **Terms framework** — Experts indemnify A2X for their content; A2X is a conduit (DMCA-style safe harbor posture)
- **Insurance marketplace** — Partnership with specialty insurers offering coverage for experts who need it
- **Content licensing** — Clear platform license terms with expert IP ownership preserved

### Enterprise-Grade Security Features

- SSO via SAML 2.0 and OIDC
- SCIM for user provisioning
- Audit log export
- Data residency options (US, EU)
- Customer-managed encryption keys (CMEK) for top tier
- DLP integration
- Custom retention policies
- Dedicated tenant option (for high-sensitivity customers)

## Proprietary Data Moat

The real enterprise moat is not the marketplace — it's the data exhaust from millions of playbook executions.

### Data Products (Phase 3+)

**Outcome Benchmarking**
- "Playbooks for responding to motion X have a 78% success rate in Missouri vs. 62% in Texas"
- Buyers: Law firms, enterprises, policy researchers, academic institutions
- Pricing: Annual subscription ($10K–$100K)

**Expert Performance Analytics**
- Objective, outcome-based expert ranking
- Buyers: Legal services buyers, professional body governance
- Pricing: Report-based ($5K–$50K) or subscription

**Regulatory Intelligence**
- Automated flagging when statutes affect playbook content
- Buyers: Compliance teams, law firms, professional bodies
- Pricing: Annual subscription ($25K–$250K)

**Practice Pattern Research**
- Aggregate insights on practice patterns, outcome disparities, regional variations
- Buyers: Academic institutions, think tanks, government agencies
- Pricing: Report and licensing

**Training Data**
- Consented, anonymized, expert-validated workflow data
- Buyers: AI labs building domain-specific models
- Pricing: Licensing deals

### Data Governance

- User consent for data use (granular opt-in)
- Anonymization before aggregation
- Research Board approval for external data use
- No sale of individual-level data ever
- Expert consent required for methodology-level data

## International Expansion Strategy

### Sequence

1. **US (home market)** — full launch, Phase 1–2
2. **UK** — Phase 3. Common law, English language, strong regulatory framework
3. **Canada** — Phase 3. Bilingual capability (English + French), similar legal system
4. **Australia / New Zealand** — Phase 3–4. English language, common law
5. **Ireland / Singapore** — Phase 4. English language, strong legal tech markets
6. **EU (Germany, France, Netherlands)** — Phase 4. GDPR-native from day one enables speed
7. **LATAM (Mexico, Brazil, Chile)** — Phase 5. Spanish/Portuguese localization
8. **APAC (Japan, South Korea, India)** — Phase 5

### Jurisdiction Infrastructure Requirements

- Jurisdiction taxonomy based on ISO 3166 (countries) and FIPS or equivalent (sub-national)
- Practice area taxonomy aligned to international standards (NAICS, ISCO-08, ICD-11, WIPO)
- Content localization pipeline — not just translation, but legal/cultural adaptation
- Regional data residency (GDPR, PIPEDA, LGPD, etc.)
- Regulatory mapping layer (which playbooks are permitted in which jurisdictions)
- Local payment methods (SEPA, local cards, bank transfers)
- Local VAT/GST handling

## Execution Principles

1. **Credibility before scale.** First institutional partnership matters more than first thousand users.
2. **Compliance as product.** Treat SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR readiness as features, not chores.
3. **Anchor first, scale later.** Don't open to self-serve expert supply until QA is proven.
4. **Verticalize deeply.** Go deep in flagship vertical before broad across verticals.
5. **Enterprise and consumer in parallel.** Don't wait to "earn" enterprise credibility via consumer scale.
6. **Build the moat intentionally.** Jurisdiction infrastructure, outcome data, and institutional relationships are the moat.
7. **Govern transparently.** Trust is a compounding asset; earn it publicly.
